# Workforce policy and evidence coverage

The base case supports about 1,327 annual construction FTE across Scottish industries for three years, followed by about 238 annual FTE in the on-site workforce and procurement suppliers. The policy problem is sequencing readiness and confirming demand, rather than treating a large capital headline as a count of permanent jobs.

## What is actually known

The Scottish Government's 2022 IO data identify industry activity and FTE coefficients. Skills England's 2025 UK staffing distribution provides a broad occupational proxy. The Scottish Employer Skills Survey 2024 identifies recruitment difficulties: 60% of skilled-trade vacancies were hard to fill for skill reasons. SDS records 659 Electrical Installation and 74 HVAC/refrigeration apprenticeship achievements in 2025/26. These measures concern different populations and dates. None identifies an available applicant for this project.

SDS additionally records 3,037 electrical apprentices in training and 834 starts. The achievement/leaver ratio is 659/829 = 79.5%; dividing achievements by starts would compare different cohorts. The 510 Digital Technology Technical Apprenticeship achievements cover a wider range of jobs than data-centre IT operations. Actual learning capacity needs provider-level confirmation; learner stocks and completions are not spare seats.

## Options

| Option | Scenario resource commitment | Readiness assumption | Appropriate decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Incumbent IT conversion | 60 seats × £4,000; 85% completion × 60% project matching | 6 months; 30.6 expected matches before any demand cap | Reserve places after employers identify eligible staff and confirm operational roles. Protect supervised practical learning time. |
| New electrical apprenticeship cohort | 80 seats × £18,000; published 79.5% achievement/leaver proxy × 60% matching | 48 months; misses a 36-month base launch | Treat as a regional skills investment or a later-expansion channel. Do not promise it covers the initial construction peak or opening. |
| Experienced-worker recruitment | 30 candidates × £6,000; 70% matching | 3 months; 21 expected matches, capped at 24 on-site electrical/cooling FTE | Verify competence and licensing, relocation interest and retention. Recruitment from existing firms may displace activity elsewhere. |

Costs are 2022-price planning assumptions for the stated cohort, not official funding rates, provider quotations or measured treatment costs. Cost per on-time match is an accounting ratio, not a benefit-cost ratio. An option with no on-time matches has an unavailable ratio. Options overlap in roles and cannot be summed without a distinct cohort plan and role-level caps. The model does not change its IO coefficients to make a policy option look attractive.

## Implementation and monitoring

1. Before committing money, obtain the occupational roster, shift pattern, contractor schedule, recruitment catchment and required qualifications. Confirm that on-site FTE exclude outsourced jobs.
2. Ask providers for available places, instructor availability, equipment requirements, actual course dates and completion evidence. Set a deadline tied to construction commissioning.
3. Recruit experienced trades and prepare a conversion cohort against the verified roster. Use staged procurement to avoid an unsupported single launch-date staffing peak.
4. Track starts, completions, role matches, job retention at six and twelve months, and whether people came from unemployment, training, another local employer or migration. Report people and FTE separately.
5. Reassess additionality and distribution before offering a subsidy. Public funding, utility infrastructure costs and environmental effects need a separate counterfactual appraisal.
