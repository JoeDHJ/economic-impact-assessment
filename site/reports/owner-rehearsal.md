# Owner rehearsal: explain, change and defend

This sheet helps turn an AI-assisted implementation into personally demonstrable economic judgement. No claim of personal mastery follows from the existence of the code. Work through it before using the portfolio in interviews; record your own answers and changed scenario files locally.

## Explain without the interface

1. A disclosed £300m facility funds two projects. Why can it not be entered directly as £300m of Scottish basic-price production?
2. Derive `6 × 8,760 / 1,650 = 31.85` service FTE for six continuous posts. Which operational restrictions remain outside that arithmetic?
3. With success 0.85 and annual attrition 0.12, derive expected three-year capacity per hire: `0.85 × 0.88³ = 0.5792512`. Why is a 12-month checkpoint insufficient for a 36-month service obligation?
4. Explain why integer places can create surplus expected capacity, and why the cost ledger still pays for those selected places.
5. Explain how public course funding changes the payer split without creating a second resource cost.
6. Why do wages counted as employer costs not automatically measure social opportunity cost? Why is gross GVA not a welfare benefit?

## Change one assumption at a time

Save the changed input JSON and result alongside a short paragraph explaining mechanism and scope. Do not seek a preferred statistical result; this is a conditional decision model with no hypothesis test.

## Defend a limitation

Prepare one clear response to each challenge:

- **“You used Scottish data for a local case.”** Scottish production effects stay Scottish; North Lanarkshire observations describe local context. No invented local multiplier or candidate pool is used.
- **“Your six-month training route qualifies electricians?”** No. It assumes qualified incumbents taking a refresher/conversion route. Novice qualification is a separate apprenticeship route with a longer assumed lead. Real competence and accreditation must be verified.
- **“Why leave administrative gaps?”** Critical deficits have lexicographic priority; route ceilings and retention limit total capacity. The residual gaps are part of the recommendation. A real client must decide whether other services should be marked critical.
- **“Why not select the later launch automatically?”** It changes demand peaks, readiness and discount timing. Delay/revenue/escalation costs are absent, so lower staffing pressure is not proof of greater investment value.
- **“Does the model demonstrate training works?”** It uses assumed yields. A causal study would require an assignment process, observed outcomes and a defensible comparison; the methods note describes a possible future evaluation.
