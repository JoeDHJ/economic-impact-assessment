# Capability evidence and interview demonstration

Haojian Dui · Economic Impact Assessment 0.2.0 · Independent portfolio case.

## Positioning

“I use economic reasoning and reproducible computation to turn AI investment announcements into workforce decisions. This case separates observed financing and labour context from planning assumptions, then links phased activity, task coverage and a constrained personnel programme.”

This is a completed work sample. Its strongest signal is the chain from an ambiguous decision to inspectable evidence, a working allocation model and a usable decision record. It does not stand in for a published causal study, commissioned client history or observed employer impact.

The product is strongest as an applied economic-analysis work sample. For research-heavy economist roles, pair it with a separate, genuinely identified empirical paper. Do not relabel the optimisation as causal inference.

## A two-minute demonstration

**0:00–0:25 — Decision and evidence.** “A financing announcement says approximately £300m, but that is not a basic-price capex bill. I therefore preserve it as a disclosed fact and use a separate, editable capital scenario. Announced jobs are projections with an unspecified FTE basis.” Open the evidence tab and point to the native status and scope.

**0:25–0:55 — Mechanism.** “Construction demand is phased, so the occupation peak differs from the annual average. Operating capacity comes from tasks and cover hours: six continuously staffed IT posts require substantially more than six FTE once noncoverage time is included.” Show the monthly chart and roster.

**0:55–1:25 — Decision and constraint.** “The solver first protects critical capacity, then reduces all gaps, then chooses the least-cost allocation at those coverage levels. The default mixed plan leaves 17.83 role-period FTE uncovered, while the critical gap is zero. The redeployment, hiring and contractor route caps are fully used; the transition budgets are not.” Point to the resource bars and role gaps.

**1:25–2:00 — Experiment and judgement.** Increase the employer budget alone, run and explain why the unchanged route limits can prevent better coverage. Then show that a 48-month apprenticeship misses a 36-month launch. Export the current decision brief and inputs. Conclude: “I would verify qualified candidates and service responsibilities before buying more seats. The model exposes a commitment decision; it does not certify a safe roster or estimate net welfare.”

## A five-minute technical follow-up

1. Derive one continuous-post FTE requirement and one three-year retention yield by hand.
2. Show the first and second lexicographic objectives and why cost cannot buy acceptance of a critical gap.
3. Explain why Scottish supplier FTE cannot be labeled North Lanarkshire jobs, and why resident employment is not applicant availability.
4. Separate transition budget, ongoing costs, programme present cost and guarantee exposure. Explain why no BCR is supplied.
5. Name one model limitation that could change the decision: actual incumbent capacity, monthly mentor calendars, role-specific completion rates, replacement recruitment or delay cost. Describe the data needed to improve it.

- Developed an independently reproducible AI-infrastructure workforce case combining 98-industry Scottish input-output accounts, 412 SOC2020 occupations and official local labour-market observations; reconciled phase-specific demand with cumulative FTE job-years.
- Implemented and verified a three-stage integer allocation model for training, hiring, redeployment and contracting under time, mentoring and two-payer budget constraints; delivered a local browser workspace with current-scenario exports and Python/browser numerical parity.
